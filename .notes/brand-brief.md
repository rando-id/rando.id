# Rando.id — brand brief

## The product

Rando is a contacts app sorted by the actual contexts of your life. Your address book reorganizes itself around the way you really know people: by proximity ("who's in town right now"), by circle ("the parents on Henry's baseball team"), by event, by neighborhood, by overlap. Web-first, native-first (iOS + Android), offline-first. Built around the idea that the most useful way to find someone is "where do they fit in my life right now."

## The conceptual hook

A "rando" is the slang word for a random person you don't know — or, more often, the person you sort-of know but can't quite place: the parent at every soccer game, the neighbor three houses down, the consultant you've met four times in three cities. Rando.id makes those people _findable_ — turning the half-known into the right-now-useful, through the contexts you actually live in. The ".id" reads as identity. The full name reads as "random identity, made knowable." Serendipity AND structure.

## Audience

Two overlapping shapes:

- **The mobile professional.** 25–45, multi-city friend networks, wants to know who's in town tonight.
- **The active parent.** 30–50, life organized around the kids' contexts — the baseball-team parents, the school carpool, the dance studio crowd, the neighborhood birthday-party circuit. Not all best friends; just the people you'd genuinely benefit from being able to find without scrolling through 1,800 alphabetical names.

Both are privacy-conscious and frustrated by static contact lists that don't reflect how relationships actually cluster.

## Personality

- **Contextual** — relationships happen _in_ something (a city, a season, a team). The brand reflects that.
- **Human** — people, not points on a map; warmth matters.
- **Quietly modern** — sophisticated tech, understated brand; confidence over cleverness.
- **Earned, not viral** — opt-in, mutual, trust-based.

## Voice

Direct, calm, low-bravado. We don't yell. Closer to Apple's support copy than a startup landing page.

## Visual direction (suggestions, not decisions)

- **Palette**: warm-neutral core (off-white, sand, soft graphite) + one confident accent (warm coral / earthy rust / deep forest). Avoid the every-startup-uses-cobalt pull.
- **Shapes**: circles (radii, contexts), lines (paths, edges), **nested groupings** (a team inside a city, a circle inside a season). Distance falloffs, contour lines, soft-edged clusters.
- **Iconography**: cartographic primitives (pins, contours, compass) overlapping with portrait silhouettes overlapping with situational icons (a baseball, a school doorway, a coffee cup). The interesting work is in the **overlap** — a pin that's also a face that's also a context.
- **Type**: humanist sans for body (Söhne, Inter), something with personality for display. Not Helvetica.
- **Vibes to chase**: Linear's confidence + Mapbox's cartographic vocabulary + Cabin Magazine's warmth.
- **Vibes to avoid**: Foursquare-playful, Facebook-blue social, dating-app pastel.

## What to skip

- 3D spinning globes
- Generic pin-on-map iconography
- 2018 Stripe-clone gradient blobs
- "Social network" node-and-line diagrams
- Anything that reads "stalkery" — Rando is opt-in and consent-first
